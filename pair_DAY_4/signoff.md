Before Mistire's explainer I knew Delta B = +0.3204 looked like evidence the judge learned the Tenacious rubric, but I could not describe what the scores in `ablation_results.json` would look like differently if the judge had instead learned to reward output length. What the explainer revealed: a positive Delta B is consistent with three distinct situations — rubric learning, length learning, or both entangled — and the only way to distinguish them is to run the three tests Mistire named. Test 1 checks whether chosen outputs in the 69 training pairs are systematically longer than rejected outputs using a Wilcoxon signed-rank test; if the ratio is above 1.3x and p < 0.05, the confound is present in the training data. Test 2 checks whether Spearman ρ between output word count and judge score on the 40 held-out outputs is above 0.3 — if it is, length is a real driver of the judge's scores, not the rubric. Test 3 — four hand-written adversarial pairs that hold rubric compliance and length in opposition — is the definitive check if Tests 1 and 2 are ambiguous. I now understand what a specific pattern in my existing `ablation_results.json` would look like under length bias versus rubric learning, and I have a concrete path to run Test 1 and Test 2 on my preference pairs and held-out outputs to resolve the ambiguity before Tenacious-Bench v0.2.
